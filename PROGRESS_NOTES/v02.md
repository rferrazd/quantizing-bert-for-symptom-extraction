# Conclusions

Full fine-tuning on a low-diversity dataset → this helped reduce overall errors (91 vs 179) but introduced Boundary Overreach (+23), which is a classic sign of the model overfitting to the syntactic patterns of your templates and over-extending span recognition to adjacent tokens.

## Why test/val metrics = 1.0 are not trustworthy

The synthetic dataset contains only **15 unique sentence templates**, all of the form `[preamble] + [symptom] + period`. The symptom span always occupies everything between the trigger phrase and the period — there are no post-symptom modifiers, temporal clauses, or conjunctions in any test example. Full fine-tuning lets BioBERT learn this heuristic exactly, achieving perfect scores on a test set that is structurally identical to training. The behavioral set, which contains realistic clinical sentences with temporal context, causal language, and multi-symptom structures, is the only reliable evaluation signal at this stage.

## What changed vs v01

| Error Type | v01 (head-only) | v02 (full fine-tuning) | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Irrelevant Span Mislabeling | 75 | 30 | -60% |
| Model Overgeneralization | 74 | 27 | -64% |
| Missing Expected Entity | 11 | 0 | -100% |
| Boundary Overreach | 8 | 31 | +288% |
| Tokenization Artifacts | 4 | 1 | -75% |
| BIO Sequencing Errors | 4 | 1 | -75% |
| Incorrect Polarity Assignment | 3 | 1 | -67% |
| **Total** | **179** | **91** | **-49%** |

Full fine-tuning is the right direction. The model no longer misses any expected entities and produces far fewer spurious spans. The Boundary Overreach regression is caused by the absence of post-symptom context in the training data, not by the training strategy itself.

## Decision for v03

- **Keep full fine-tuning.** The parameter update improves representation learning across all error types except Boundary Overreach.
- **Fix the dataset**, not the model. The primary bottleneck is the 15-template synthetic corpus. v03 training data must include:

  - *Increase template count dramatically* — aim for 100–200+ distinct sentence structures. Use an LLM (GPT-4/Claude) to rewrite your existing 15 templates into varied syntactic forms: complex sentences, negations, conjunctions, embedded clauses, passive voice, patient-reported phrasing.
  - *Add negative examples* — sentences that contain medical-sounding words but no symptoms, to reduce irrelevant span mislabeling (still your #2 error at 30).
  - *Add hard boundary examples* — sentences where symptoms are adjacent to non-symptom spans, specifically to teach the model where to stop. This directly targets your Boundary Overreach problem.
